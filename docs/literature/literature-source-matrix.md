# Thesis Literature Source Matrix

## Purpose

This document records the verified academic literature selected for the
Bachelor thesis "AI-Assisted Personalized Study Planning System".

The literature is selected to support five theoretical areas:

- self-regulated learning and time management;
- personalized and adaptive learning;
- educational recommender systems;
- artificial intelligence and explainability in higher education;
- generative AI and human-AI interaction.

---

# 1. Self-Regulated Learning and Study Planning

## Zimmerman (2002)

Zimmerman, B. J. (2002).
Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview.
Theory Into Practice, 41(2), 64–70.
DOI: 10.1207/S15430421TIP4102_2

### Thesis relevance

Provides a foundational explanation of self-regulated learning.

Useful for establishing that effective learners actively regulate goals,
strategies, behaviour and progress rather than depending entirely on
external instruction.

### Intended use

Section 1.4:
Self-Regulated Learning, Time Management and Study Planning.

Supports the theoretical connection between student self-regulation and
the thesis system's goals, planning, progress tracking and replanning.

---

## Broadbent and Poon (2015)

Broadbent, J., & Poon, W. L. (2015).
Self-regulated learning strategies & academic achievement in online
higher education learning environments: A systematic review.
The Internet and Higher Education, 27, 1–13.
DOI: 10.1016/j.iheduc.2015.04.007

### Thesis relevance

Systematic review of self-regulated learning strategies in online higher
education.

The review identified time management, metacognition, effort regulation
and critical thinking among strategies positively related to academic
achievement.

### Intended use

Section 1.4.

Provides direct higher-education evidence for treating time management
as an important part of student self-regulation.

---

## Panadero (2017)

Panadero, E. (2017).
A Review of Self-regulated Learning: Six Models and Four Directions
for Research.
Frontiers in Psychology, 8, 422.
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00422

### Thesis relevance

Reviews six major models of self-regulated learning and explains SRL as
including cognitive, metacognitive, behavioural, motivational and
emotional processes.

### Intended use

Section 1.4.

Provides a broader theoretical foundation for describing planning,
monitoring and adaptation as components of self-regulated learning.

---

## Liu, Ma and Jia (2026)

Liu, B., Ma, P., & Jia, F. (2026).
Systematic review and meta-analysis of the impact of time management
on college students' learning outcomes.
Frontiers in Psychology, 17, 1700298.
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1700298

### Thesis relevance

Recent systematic review and meta-analysis examining time management
and learning outcomes among college students.

The study analysed 31 studies with a combined sample of 13,506
participants and reported a moderate positive association between time
management and learning outcomes.

The reported heterogeneity was high, so the result should be described
as an association rather than evidence that time management alone
causes better academic performance.

### Intended use

Section 1.4.

Provides recent empirical support for the relevance of structured time
management in higher education.

# 2. Personalized and Adaptive Learning

## Zhong (2023)

Zhong, L. (2023).
A systematic review of personalized learning in higher education:
learning content structure, learning materials sequence, and learning
readiness support.
Interactive Learning Environments, 31(10), 7053–7073.
DOI: 10.1080/10494820.2022.2061006

### Thesis relevance

Systematic review of personalized learning design in higher education.

The review examined 41 studies and identified increasing interest in
personalized learning while also noting limitations in the maturity and
integration of existing approaches.

### Intended use

Sections 1.1 and 1.5.

Supports the discussion of personalized learning and helps position the
thesis within the broader higher-education personalization literature.

---

## du Plooy, Casteleijn and Franzsen (2024)

du Plooy, E., Casteleijn, D., & Franzsen, D. (2024).
Personalized adaptive learning in higher education: A scoping review
of key characteristics and impact on academic performance and
engagement.
Heliyon, 10(21), e39630.
DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e39630

### Thesis relevance

Recent review focused specifically on personalized adaptive learning in
higher education.

The review discusses personalization, adaptation, academic performance,
student engagement, and implementation limitations.

### Intended use

Sections 1.1, 1.4 and 1.5.

Particularly relevant to the adaptive replanning component because the
thesis updates study recommendations as the student's progress changes.

# 3. Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education

## Crompton and Burke (2023)

Crompton, H., & Burke, D. (2023).
Artificial intelligence in higher education: the state of the field.
International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education,
20, Article 22.
DOI: 10.1186/s41239-023-00392-8

### Thesis relevance

Systematic review of 138 studies concerning AI in higher education
between 2016 and 2022.

### Intended use

Section 1.3.

Updates the earlier Zawacki-Richter et al. (2019) AI-in-higher-
education review and provides more recent context for AI-supported
educational applications.

---

## Khosravi et al. (2022)

Khosravi, H., Shum, S. B., Chen, G., Conati, C., Tsai, Y.-S.,
Kay, J., Knight, S., Martinez-Maldonado, R., Sadiq, S.,
& Gašević, D. (2022).
Explainable Artificial Intelligence in education.
Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 3, Article 100074.
DOI: 10.1016/j.caeai.2022.100074

### Thesis relevance

Introduces the XAI-ED framework and argues that educational AI has
distinctive transparency, agency and explanation requirements.

### Intended use

Section 1.3 and the discussion of the hybrid architecture.

Supports the decision to retain transparent deterministic scheduling
logic and readable scheduling rationales instead of allowing AI to act
as an opaque timetable generator.

---

# 4. Generative AI and Human-AI Interaction

## Lo (2023)

Lo, C. K. (2023).
What Is the Impact of ChatGPT on Education? A Rapid Review of the
Literature.
Education Sciences, 13(4), 410.
DOI: 10.3390/educsci13040410

### Thesis relevance

Early rapid review of generative conversational AI in education.

Identifies potential educational assistance applications while also
highlighting risks including inaccurate generated information.

### Intended use

Section 1.3.

Supports the decision to use generative AI as an advisory layer rather
than the authoritative source of scheduling constraints.

---

## Liang et al. (2026)

Liang, Z., Yang, K., Sha, L., Gašević, D., Yan, L., & Chen, G. (2026).
A systematic review of generative AI in education: Empirical insights
from a human-AI interaction perspective.
British Journal of Educational Technology, 57, 1221–1271.
DOI: 10.1111/bjet.70055

### Thesis relevance

Recent systematic review of 56 empirical studies of generative AI in
education.

The review examines educational GenAI from a human-AI interaction
perspective, including different relationships between human control
and AI automation.

### Intended use

Sections 1.3 and 1.5.

Strongly supports the thesis hybrid architecture in which deterministic
application logic maintains control over hard scheduling decisions and
AI provides interpretation and recommendation.
